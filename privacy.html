Privacy notice

Last updated 21 November 2026

Black Hole Light Paths is a free educational simulation run by Nader Eloshaiker as a personal, non-commercial project. This notice explains what data the site collects and the choices you have.

The simulation

The simulation runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you do with the controls is sent anywhere or stored on a server. The site has no accounts, forms or comments.

Google Analytics (only with your permission)

If you choose Allow analytics, the site loads Google Analytics 4 to count visits and see which parts of the page people use. Until you choose, and if you choose No thanks, Google Analytics is not loaded at all and sets no cookies.

When it is allowed, Google Analytics collects:

  • pages viewed, scrolling and clicks on links to other sites
  • your browser, device type, screen size and language
  • your approximate location (city or region), worked out from your IP address; Google Analytics 4 does not store IP addresses
  • the website or search that brought you here

This data is used only to understand how the simulation is used and to improve it. It is not used for advertising, it is not sold, and Google signals and ad personalisation are not used. Google processes the data on our behalf under the Google Ads Data Processing Terms; see Google's privacy policy for how Google handles data.

These cookies are set only after you choose Allow analytics. Your choice itself is remembered in your browser's local storage (bhlp-consent) for 12 months, so you are not asked on every visit. That entry never leaves your browser.

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, the site treats it as No thanks and does not ask.

Changing your mind

Use Cookie settings at the bottom of the simulator page at any time. If you withdraw permission, Google Analytics stops collecting and its cookies are deleted from your browser.

Other services

  • GitHub Pages hosts the site. GitHub may log your IP address for security and to operate the service; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Google Fonts supplies the typefaces. Your browser requests them from Google's servers, which receive your IP address to deliver them.

Your rights and contact

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access or delete data about you. Because the site cannot identify you, the simplest way to remove analytics data is to withdraw permission, which deletes the cookies that link your visits. For any question about this notice, please open an issue on GitHub.
